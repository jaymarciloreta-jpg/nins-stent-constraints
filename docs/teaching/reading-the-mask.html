    Images are numbers on a grid

    A CT volume is a 3D array of numbers. Each cell is a voxel, a small box of tissue with a known size called its spacing, for example 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.6 mm. Spacing is often different along each axis. Every millimetre the software reports comes from this geometry, so wrong spacing corrupts every measurement. Correct resampling keeps the physical scale, but interpolation and the new resolution can shift apparent boundaries and the measurements taken from them.

    In CT the number in each voxel is a Hounsfield unit (HU), an attenuation scale fixed to water at 0 and air at about −1000.1 Because the scale is physical, a threshold such as "air is below −400 HU" means roughly the same thing on every calibrated scanner. Some reconstructions also pad the area outside the field of view with values such as −2000 or −3024. Those values are not tissue, and an automated method has to know to ignore them.

    MRI also stores a number per voxel, but that number has no fixed unit. It depends on the sequence, the coil and the scanner, and it is only meaningful relative to other voxels in the same scan. Section 4 shows why that matters.

    Windowing is a display setting. Width and level choose which HU range is spread across black-to-white. Changing the window changes what you see. It does not change the numbers, and a threshold segmentation does not care what window you were viewing in.

    02

    Classical methods: rules you could write on a whiteboard

    Before deep learning, segmentation was built from a few simple operations. They are still the backbone of most clinical tools, including the threshold proposal in the NINS pilot panel, and each one has a failure mode a clinician can learn to spot.

    Thresholding
    Label every voxel whose value falls in a range. The pilot panel proposes air as −1024 HU up to a chosen upper limit, and bone as anything above a chosen lower limit, inside a region of interest. It is fast and transparent. It knows nothing about anatomy: all air in the box is "air", including sinus or external air, and all dense voxels are "bone".
    Region growing / grow from seeds
    Start from voxels you mark and spread to neighbours that look similar. 3D Slicer's Grow from seeds takes seeds for several structures at once and lets them compete for the voxels in between.2,3 It respects connectivity, so it can separate two air spaces of the same HU, but it leaks through any gap in a wall, including a gap that exists only because the wall partial-volumed away.
    Morphology
    Grow (dilate) or shrink (erode) a mask by a set radius. A closing (grow, then shrink) fills small holes. An opening (shrink, then grow) removes thin strands. Both smooth the mask, and both can create or erase a foramen a few voxels wide.
    Connected components
    Group touching voxels into separate islands, then keep or delete islands by size. "Keep the largest island" removes noise specks. It also deletes any real structure that is not connected to the biggest one.
    Atlas registration
    Warp a labeled reference scan (an atlas) onto the new patient and carry its labels across. Using many atlases and voting between them is more robust.4 It handles structures that have no unique intensity, but it fails when the patient's anatomy differs from every atlas, as after surgery or with large anatomic variants.

    03

    Reconstruction kernels, partial volume and thin bone

    The scanner does not photograph tissue. It reconstructs each slice from projections, and the reconstruction kernel (filter) chosen at that step trades sharpness against noise. A standard or soft-tissue kernel smooths: low noise, blurred edges. A bone or sharp kernel preserves fine detail: crisp edges, more noise, and a small overshoot on each side of a high-contrast edge (a bright halo on the bone side, a dark dip next to it).

    Kernels change the numbers, not just the look. In the same femur scans, volumetric bone density measured about 6.7% lower on a standard kernel than on a bone-sharpening kernel.6 In coronary CT, measured plaque attenuation shifted with both kernel and slice thickness.7 A fixed HU threshold therefore does not mean the same thing across kernels.

    Partial volume is the larger problem for thin bone. A voxel's value is roughly the average of everything inside it, smeared further by the scanner's blur. A 0.25 mm plate of 1400 HU cortex sitting between air (−1000) and soft tissue cannot fill a 0.5 mm voxel, so its voxels read far lower than real bone. Below the system's spatial resolution, thin bone loses peak contrast and its apparent thickness becomes unreliable: depending on the boundary method and threshold it can look broadened, narrowed or absent.5 In this project, the posterior SPF rim on a 0.5 mm scan was noted to cap near 227 HU, so a 300 HU bone threshold looks straight through it and reports the foramen as open. For this reason the team chose the bone-kernel reconstruction as the source for the setup cases.18

    Two lessons follow. First, no single HU threshold is correct for thin bone, because its value depends on thickness, on its neighbours, on the kernel, and even on where the voxel grid happens to fall (try the offset slider). Second, the sharper kernel recovers more of the rim's true contrast but adds noise, so a lower threshold on a bone kernel starts to pick up specks. Methods that look for a local rise above the surrounding tissue, rather than an absolute HU, are more robust here, and this project's legacy aperture tool uses that approach.18

    04

    Why MRI is harder to segment automatically

    No absolute units
    The same tissue gives different numbers on different scanners, coils and protocols, and even between two visits on the same scanner. A threshold copied from one scan has no meaning on the next.
    Bias field
    Receive-coil sensitivity makes the image brighter near the coil and darker away from it. This smooth, multiplicative shading means one tissue can be bright on one side of the head and dim on the other. Algorithms such as N4 estimate and divide out this field before segmentation.8
    Sequence dependence
    Contrast is a choice. Fat is bright on T1, inflamed mucosa and fluid are bright on T2, and fast-flowing blood can be dark on both. A model or rule built on one sequence does not transfer to another.
    Bone and air look alike
    Cortical bone and air are both near signal void on conventional sequences. A thin bony plate between airway and soft tissue is nearly invisible against the air beside it, which is one reason CT is the standard for sinonasal bony anatomy.
    Normalization
    Before any rule or model is applied, MR intensities are usually rescaled to a common reference. Histogram standardization maps each scan's intensity landmarks onto a standard scale.9 Even then, intensities are only comparable within one sequence type.

    Deep learning: a model that learned from labeled examples

    A convolutional neural network (CNN) slides millions of small learned filters across the image. Early filters respond to edges and textures. Later ones combine those into larger patterns such as "turbinate" or "nasal cavity". Nobody writes these filters by hand. The network learns them by adjusting its weights until its output matches labels that people drew on training scans.

    The U-Net is the standard design for segmentation.10 An encoder repeatedly shrinks the image to capture context: what region this is, what is nearby. A decoder expands it back to full resolution to place boundaries. Skip connections pass fine detail from each encoder level straight to the matching decoder level, so precise edges are not lost in the compression.

    Labels are the curriculum

    The model can only reproduce what its training labels contain. If the annotators traced the nasal cavity at a particular boundary convention, the model learns that convention. If no one labeled the sphenopalatine foramen, the model has no concept of it and will never output it. Label quality, consistency between annotators, and the cases chosen for training set an upper limit on what the model can do.

    Two examples you will meet

    nnU-Net is a self-configuring framework. Given a labeled dataset, it chooses its own preprocessing, patch size, network depth and training schedule, and it matched or beat specialized methods across many public segmentation challenges.11 It is a recipe for training, not a pretrained model.

    TotalSegmentator is a set of pretrained nnU-Net models for CT. The original study used 1204 routine CT examinations (1082 for training, 57 for validation, 65 for testing) to build a model of 104 structures, which reported a mean test-set Dice of 0.943. That result does not establish performance of the later nasal-cavity task, or at the SPF rim.12 Later releases added specialized tasks. The head_glands_cavities task lists right and left nasal cavities, nasopharynx and hard palate.13 In the NINS pilot panel it is an optional adapter that restricts the threshold air proposal, while bone stays threshold-based. It has no SPF label, and its real-world inference on pilot scans is not yet validated.18

    A deep-learning mask always looks finished. That is its most dangerous property. Smoothness is not evidence of accuracy.

    06

    How results are judged

    Dice
    Overlap between the automatic mask and a reference mask: twice the shared volume divided by the sum of both volumes.14 1.0 is perfect, 0 is no overlap. It is dominated by the bulk of a structure, so a large structure can score high while its edges are wrong.
    Hausdorff distance
    The single worst boundary disagreement, in mm. Sensitive to any local error, including a single stray voxel. The 95th-percentile version (HD95) ignores the worst 5% of boundary points to reduce that sensitivity.15
    Surface distance
    The average distance between the two boundaries, or the fraction of boundary within a set tolerance (surface Dice). Surface metrics track how much a person would need to redraw.16
    Correction burden
    Time or voxels a trained reviewer needs to fix the mask to an acceptable standard. This is the measure that matters for a workflow, and it is the comparison the pilot plans between the threshold and TotalSegmentator-initialized paths.18

    The Metrics Reloaded consensus recommends choosing metrics from the clinical question, and in particular pairing an overlap metric with a boundary metric when boundary position matters.17

    How to review an autosegmentation

    Use this before any measurement taken from an automatic mask enters the study dataset. Completing it does not make a measurement suitable for clinical decisions. The ticks below are only for your own use and are not saved anywhere.

    Why three planes

    A mask is a 3D object, and one slice shows only one cut through it. A wall can look intact in one plane and broken in another, and a gap in the mask can be real anatomy or a segmentation miss. The only way to tell is to move through the volume and compare the mask with the gray image underneath it in each plane.

    Hands-on: the NINS Pilot panel

    The NINS Pilot panel is the 3D Slicer module this project is building (version 0.4.0). It puts Lessons 1–7 into one guided workflow. You verify the source, place an independent reference, generate candidate air and bone masks, correct them, review the geometry, then measure and export. Start with the panel's synthetic demonstration before you open any real scan.18

    Install and open it

    1. Clone or download the whole nins-stent-constraints repository. Keep its NINSPilot and scripts folders together.
    2. In Slicer, open Settings › Modules › Additional module paths, add the repository's NINSPilot folder, and restart Slicer.
    3. Choose Research › NINS Pilot, then Load synthetic demonstration.

    What the numbers mean

    The panel measures from the rim you placed, not from an automatic SPF finder. Each output is research geometry with a stated limitation.

    OutputDefinition and limitation
    Aperture areaArea of the ordered rim polygon projected onto its least-squares plane. Self-intersecting or collinear rims are refused.
    Minimum FeretMinimum distance between parallel supporting lines of the projected rim's convex hull.
    Maximum FeretMaximum pairwise distance on that convex hull.
    Plane residualsRMS and maximum distance of rim points from the fitted plane. Reported alongside dimensions, with no pass/fail cutoff.
    Local surface-to-SPF distanceShortest 3D distance from the SPF reference to the reviewed local patch. Not SPG distance or stimulation reach.

    Running a real setup case

    The same steps on a real scan, condensed from the panel guide, with the lesson behind each one.

    1. Load the verified original CT, enter coded case and reviewer IDs, choose Setup and a secure study directory outside the repository, then Start session. Lesson 1: the source is matched by voxel fingerprint and geometry, not by name.
    2. Tick each source check only after confirming it. Setup requires native submillimeter sampling; Challenge allows technical exploration outside that sampling, but the panel still requires every source check, including noncontrast CT and verified HU. Never tick an untrue assertion or relabel resampled data as native. Lessons 1 and 3
    3. Mark each side assessable or record why not.
    4. Place one SPF point and an ordered RIM curve on each assessable side, before showing any candidate. A second rater works in a separate session. Lesson 6: an independent reference
    5. Fit the ROI around the nasal region with margin, and generate candidates with one method. The uncorrected masks are saved first. Lessons 2 and 5
    6. Correct the air and bone masks. Look for sinus or external air in the air mask, thin walls missing from the bone mask, and artificial ROI edges. If you smooth, record it. Lessons 2, 3 and 7
    7. Optionally place CONTACT, AIR and ROUTE points, build each local patch, and inspect it in slices and 3D.
    8. Tick the review box, Accept current geometry, then Calculate measurements. Any later edit needs acceptance again. Lesson 7
    9. Export to a new run directory, and keep the whole study directory out of the public repository.

    The panel uses your reviewed rim directly. It does not find the SPF on its own, and its numbers are research geometry, not a fabrication specification.
